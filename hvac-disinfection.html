HVAC Disinfection FAQs

Is HVAC Disinfection the Same as Air Duct Cleaning?

No. Air Duct Cleaning removes physical buildup. Disinfection is a separate, product-specific treatment. The EPA explains the differences between cleaning, sanitizing, and disinfecting.

Does Every HVAC System Need Disinfection?

No. A treatment should address a specific condition and be suitable for the system. It should not be an automatic add-on to every cleaning. Review the EPA’s guidance on chemical treatments in air ducts before approving the work.

Can the Same Disinfectant Be Used on Every HVAC Surface?

No. Suitability depends on the material and the product’s permitted uses. Bare metal, insulation, and other components should not be treated as interchangeable. The EPA’s duct-treatment guidance explains important limitations.

Will Disinfection Alone Resolve a Mold or Moisture Problem?

Not necessarily. An unresolved moisture problem can allow mold to return, so treatment should not replace investigating the cause. Read the EPA’s moisture-control guidance, then contact us to discuss an appropriate next step.

What Should I Ask About the Proposed Disinfection Product?

Ask for its name, permitted surfaces, intended use, and application instructions. An EPA registration does not establish suitability for every HVAC component. The EPA’s disinfectant-label guidance explains what the label establishes.

Is Fogging or Spraying Always an Approved Application Method?

No. The product label must support the proposed application method. EPA does not recommend using these methods when the required directions are absent. Review the EPA’s guidance on fogging and electrostatic application.

Can People and Pets Stay Nearby During Treatment?

That depends on the product and application. Get preparation, ventilation, and re-entry instructions before the appointment rather than assuming everyone can remain nearby. The EPA’s product-label guidance emphasizes following the specific directions and precautions.
